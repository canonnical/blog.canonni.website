Throughout human history, technology has always been evolving and adapting to new needs, whether to kill or to benefit. Most of these innovations are straightforward, such as a hammer, a plate, or a box. On the other hand, complex inventions, such as the steam engine, have greatly progressed civilization and have had lasting impacts and sent shockwaves forward in time. We feel thousands of those shockwaves a day and use them to support our lives while commemorating their inventors centries after their death. However, not all of these shockwaves are created by sophisticated machines. For example, one significant shockwave is created by the wheel, a simple circle, or is it?

Wheels were first invented in Mesopotamia, near modern-day Iraq. Before the invention, farmers and merchants had to carry goods by hand, a cumbersome process. Nobody knows the name of the inventor, but we do know one thing for sure: they were a genius for their time. 

Put down your thousand-dollar aluminum box with silicone inside, and imagine this: on a hot summer day in Ur, a man, a normal man, shouts, ‘Eureka!’ Well, he almost definitely shouted something else, as Greek wasn’t formed back then. Anyways, whatever he shouted, others heard, and hundreds flocked to his house, where they saw the man, exhausted from several days of work, holding a circle carved out of stone. The crowd, to say the least, was shocked.

A villager broke the silence, shouting “What does it do? Show us!” in ancient Mesopotamian languages. The crowd echoed the demand, “Show it!”, they screamed. Slowly, the man attached the circle to a log, before putting that log under a plank. The plank started moving by itself. “Miraculous!”, the farmer shouted. “So, what do you want to name this?”, the scribe asked, ready to make a sketch for thousands of generations later to view and admire.

*“How about ‘Wheel’?” “Mhm… I like that, nice and catchy” “Viva la Wheel!”*

By closing our eyes and imagining, we see a fictitious past. Did any of that really happen, probably not, but that’s a theory, and that’s what history needs. If we widen our scope, we can see countless ‘Wheel’ moments in history, where we have the result in front of us, and we leave it to historians to fill in the blanks.

So, as the great cart of history rolls on, and as more and more ‘Wheels’ are created, we should appreciate the inventors. Though they may seem like lunatics, though their ideals might not be mainstream, they are our only hammers, and we have to rely on them to send shockwaves to the future.

> “And while some may see them as the crazy ones, we see genius. Because the people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world, are the ones who do.” 
> -Steve Jobs
